Muras

What was left from our ancestors, kept as sacred.

Scroll

As I grew up in a Muslim family, most of what I know is related to that. And we have old traditions as Kyrgyz people. From the traditions that were left from my ancestors, we see everything as sacred: the sun, the rain, nature, the river, and the lake I live near.

01

The Horned Mother Deer

Мүйүздүү Эне Бугу

  1. Kyrgyzmy people
  2. Buguthe deer · the biggest clan
  3. Yrysraythe people next to the lake
  4. MeI am Bugu

We believe that our nation was saved by the deer. We have a legend, passed down by our ancestors, about the Horned Mother Deer, and that is what we believe is most sacred. It explains the survival of the early people, and therefore it had a big influence on our Kyrgyz tribal confederation. That is why the biggest clan of the Kyrgyz people is called Bugu, which means the deer.

I am Bugu. Over time it had sub-clans, and I am Yrysray, the people next to the lake.

I learned about it at school from my Kyrgyz language teacher. It really excited me, and that is where I started to learn the history of the Kyrgyz people: how we used to span all the way to the Yenisei River, battling, and how our grandfathers were the real heroes and the soldiers who protected us and our neighbours.

03

A Summer in Kyrchyn

No phone, no electricity. Water from the river, heat from the fire.

I got to live by the mountains, very far from civilization, in a place called Kyrchyn, one of the most famous places in Kyrgyzstan right now. I used to live there during the summer. We had a lot of horses and sheep, and because everything is green and the grass is enough for everybody, during that time I understood and got to know my own culture, what is sacred or not, and how close we are to nature.

It was a wonderful experience, completely devoted to nature itself.

    04

    We do not say wolf. We say “dog-eater.”

    One of the things I noticed is how we call a predator not by its name. Like the wolf: even though we have a direct word for it, we used to call it the dog-eater, the killer, being afraid that they can come at any time and attack our animals. We have had cases where we lost many sheep right by our house.

    We know they will come once in a while, and we see it as a gift. Rather let them take an animal than a child or a person.

    We know that they are hungry; that is why they had to come down.

    05

    The Komuz

    Three strings. Pluck each one.

    When I play the komuz, I feel how my ancestors used to live for thousands of years before me: learning more about them, how they used to feel, and what they did day to day.

    My mother used to tell me how her father was really good at it. She missed him a lot, because she lost him during her childhood. I never saw my grandpa from my mother's side, and that is why I was inspired to learn.

    I went to music school and finished my music degree there. Seeing my mom happy made me happy.

    I used to play at the ceremonies of our relatives and at any functions, entertaining everybody. Being the joy of the community helped me realise how I could bring joy to everybody.

    The elders praised me. They used to call me a future akyn, a very respected person. They saw it as sacred too.

    06

    At the Table

    Every family member has a dedicated part.

    In our family gatherings, in the room or in the yurt, the elders have to sit at the top. When they are given food, they are given the most respected part of the sheep, horse or cow, dedicated just for them. Every family member has a dedicated part of the animal that is boiled. Men and women get different parts, and if someone mistakenly gives the wrong one, it is considered disrespect.

    Tap a name

    This taught everybody respect for each other and for the elders from childhood. I have seen all of this in person.

    Nooruz & sumolok

    Nooruz is a new year for us, because our calendar was different. We make sumolok, which is made from a lot of things, in respect of a mother who had nothing to feed her children. She used everything she had and boiled it for three days and nights, singing to her children that it would be ready soon. Sometimes it even has stones. I see it as very caring.

    Because my mom is the wife of the eldest son, she has helpers, the wives of the younger brothers, and they usually cook it.

    Weddings

    We have traditions like the wedding, where both families celebrate together.

    07

    A Ceremony I Question

    But there are ceremonies that I believe are unjust and very unfair.

    For example, if you liked a girl, you could take her in an arranged marriage, which was very popular. My grandmother was kidnapped and became a wife for my grandpa. She used to tell me about it.

    She was not angry. She told me that she, her friends and her relatives all came to their houses this way, as the culture was, because the duty of a man was to sustain the family and make sure everybody was happy. So she was fine with that.

    It was very popular, but now everybody is against it. Just fifty years ago it was different. When I think about it, it was crazy.

    Now we oppose it, because the thought that my daughter or my sister could be kidnapped makes me feel bad.

    Far From Home

    Kyrchyn ≈ 9,800 km Tacoma
    Баткан күн кайра чыгат, бирок жоголгон Мекен кайтып келбейт. Ошондуктан ата журтту көздүн карегиндей сактоо ыйык парз. Kyrgyz saying

    Being very far from my own country, where I was born, sometimes makes me question: maybe I am lost, very far away; maybe I came too far from Kyrgyzstan.

    But one thing is true: I miss the people, the nature and the culture.

    The distance has changed how I see my country, and how I see the world, where people have to leave their own town and their family to seek knowledge and chase their dreams. That breaks my heart.

    But being very far strengthens my feelings for my hometown and my country, and I fall in love with it even more.

    09

    Muras

    Everything I have said here changed how I view the world, where nature and humans blend into one and live together in peace. Living with my parents and relatives, learning all these things from the elders, I really do care about my traditions, what we see as sacred, and nature.

    I care about nature and traditions because they bring peace to the community, trust and respect, keeping everybody on the same page, and because we keep as sacred what was left to us by our ancestors: Muras.
